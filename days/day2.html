--- Day 2: Lights, Machines and Wattage ---

The elves this year have decided to make their Christmas more energy efficient to reduce environmental impact and power grid issues as a result of making so many gifts for millions of people. Instead of using the power grid, they will use solar panels to power the North Pole's gift-building machines and Christmas lights.

The elves need to find out how many solar panels to order. If a row of plugs receives too little power, lights and machines will fail to work properly, and the machines might produce LEGOs with manufacturing defects!

--- Power each row ---

Each input row contains exactly 30 digits, with no spaces. Each digit describes one connected component:

  • 1: a string of Christmas lights, consuming 6 W.
  • 2: a gift machine, consuming 234 W.

Each solar panel provides 500 W of capacity. Rows cannot share panels because of how the box is wired. For each row, find the smallest whole number of panels whose combined capacity meets or exceeds that row's demand. Unused capacity is allowed; it is not extra power forced into the components.

Add the number of panels needed by each row to find the total order. Round up each row separately, before adding. 1000 W = 1 kW.

--- Example ---

111222222112222112212121212121
212212211211212121222121111211

The first row contains 13 light strings and 17 machines. It uses 13 × 6 + 17 × 234 = 4056 W (4.056 kW), so it needs 9 panels. Eight panels provide only 4000 W of capacity.

The second row contains 16 light strings and 14 machines. It uses 16 × 6 + 14 × 234 = 3372 W (3.372 kW), so it needs 7 panels.

This example setup needs 9 + 7 = 16 solar panels.

How many solar panels must the elves order for all rows in your input?
