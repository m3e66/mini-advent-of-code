--- Day 67: The Elves Have 67 Dollars ---

The elves have 67 dollars, and their bank account statement has arrived in a rather unusual format. They need to find their current balance before installing a supercharger upgrade on Santa's sleigh without ending up in debt.

The upgrade costs $5,000. The elves want money left over, so they will buy it only if their final balance is strictly greater than $5,000. Exactly $5,000 is not enough under their rule.

--- Decode the statement ---

Start with a balance of 67. Read the statement from left to right. It contains four symbols:

  • { increases the balance by 1.
  • } decreases the balance by 1.
  • [ opens a doubling window over the next two symbol positions. Any money change in that window is doubled. Overlapping windows multiply: two windows make a change worth 4.
  • ~ skips everything through the next two money changes ({ or }). Skipped money changes do nothing. Any [ or ~ encountered while skipping is ignored and does not extend the skip.

Doubling changes only the value of { and }; it never changes a modifier's duration or strength. Every symbol position, including a modifier or skipped symbol, uses a position in an existing doubling window. A modifier at the end simply has no effect beyond the end of the statement.

--- Overlapping doubles ---

The first [ covers the second [ and the {. The second [ covers both money changes. Thus { adds 4 and } subtracts 2: a net change of +2, leaving a final balance of 69.

--- Skipped changes ---

{{[}}~[{}

The first {{ adds 2. Then [}} subtracts 4. Finally, ~ ignores the following [ and skips both money changes. The net change is −2, leaving a final balance of 65. Neither example can fund the upgrade.

Your input is one line of 5,001 symbols, with no spaces. The statement may end in an unfinished modifier window; process only the symbols present.
